The schema now has a risk field. It was inferred from abs(direction) > 0.3 or magnitude > 0.5 — a proxy built from two fields answering different questions, so a shock of unclear direction scored zero on the metric weighted 8×. Asked directly since 4 Aug.
Model and prompt questions both settled. Sonnet 5 landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor, so the model stays Haiku. Re-graded with risk measured honestly, theta returns 30.0% risk recall against a 45% floor — so the prompt stays current too.
Read the numbers above as a new baseline, not an improvement. The grader changed and every variant's shared key block now asks for risk, which moved the other fields too. current's 233→177 is the measurement becoming honest, not the scorer getting better.
Still open: calibration. Stated confidence does not track accuracy, and item confidence is the weight in direction × confidence aggregation — so the corpus is weighted by a noisy number. theta was markedly better here (rising to 97.1%), which is the one reason to revisit it.

Three things decide which 40 get sent

  • Source weight — macro 1.2, econ 1.1, news/wire 1.0, plus a 1.3 ranking nudge for macro/econ
  • Recency decay — weight halves every 72h, per item. Before this shipped, a 98-hour-old “U.S. and Iran halt attacks” outranked a 23-hour-old “attacks resume” on the same conflict.
  • Diversity cap — no source type may exceed 60% of the prompt

IV/RV below 1 means options price less movement than SPX has delivered — selling insurance below the recent claim rate, the seller's edge running backwards. At 0.746 the premium_edge term costs 0.038 of a 0.05 gate. Remove it alone and all nine of 7 August's candidates clear.

The only stage that reads what happened last week

Every prediction whose windows have elapsed is settled into prediction_outcomes — written once, never revised — and a correction is refitted from the whole series each cycle. Two claims, two windows:

tails
Largest excursion each way over paper_hold_days, in expected moves. Past 1.0 EM is a breach — the synthesis prompt’s own wording.
direction
Sign of the forward return over horizon_days.

No new data feed: the price series is the predictions themselves, each of which already carried market_context.price.

It corrected nothing, on purpose. 43 windows sampled 45 minutes apart across 14 observed days is not 43 observations — at a 4-day window it deflates to about three. With zero breaches in three effective windows the upper bound on the true rate is still 0.79, so a stated 0.35 sits comfortably inside it and stands. Read n_eff, never the row count.
Loosening is harder than tightening. A correction may go to the point estimate when raising a tail estimate, but only to the near edge of the interval when lowering one. Understating a tail costs the account; overstating one costs a trade — and a quiet fortnight is precisely the setup for the tail that a naive fit would teach you to sell.
Confidence is measured and never applied. Inverted on two independent samples. Flipping it with a factor would bury the finding, and confidence_gate was placed at the 75th percentile of that number’s raw distribution — rescaling its input is the July gate failure one layer down.

5. price
min_edge_score, POP, RoR, liquidity — the binding constraint, and now tested
The stage finally ran end to end. On 7 August event_risk was false for the first time in the function's life, so the delta cap ran at 0.30 instead of 0.20. Every one of nine cycles produced a candidate, against 11 of the previous 190 — and put verticals reached the ranking on all nine, a line that had never once appeared.
Every candidate was a call spread, against a model reading UP. 27 of 27 all time. The put side is no longer filtered out before it competes; it competes and loses, because put skew flattens the OTM price curve so a fixed-width put spread collects less per point of width.
What stops it now is the edge score, and one term inside it. Best edge 0.013–0.031 against 0.05. Decomposed: ev_ratio +0.028, buffer +0.047, alignment −0.017, premium_edge −0.038. That last term alone is the difference between nine trades and none — and on 9 August it was measured at roughly 1.5× too small, not too large.
Instrumented 9 Aug, deliberately inert. Each candidate now carries pop_real and premium_edge_measured — POP repriced on realised vol at that strike and DTE. Neither touches edge. On this measurement 23 of the 25 candidates ever produced are negative-EV, which makes this stage's refusal the correct answer rather than a blockage.
the signal arm has still never reached here

Two arms, only one of which trades

signal
Trades only when all gates clear. Has never opened a position.
baseline
Sells a ~0.15-delta put spread daily, ignoring the model entirely. The control the model is measured against.

Exits

Close at 2× the credit received, or after 4 days, whichever comes first. Marked against a chain spanning the open expiries — its own fetch since 4 Aug, separate from the scan chain.

Marking was broken from the start, and silently. The scan chain spans dte_min..dte_max from today, so a position left it within days of opening and mark_spread() returned None. manage() skips an unmarkable position before testing the exits — so it was not merely unpriced, it was unclosable. The 4-day time exit never fired once. Fixed 4 Aug.
The arms are not comparable. Baseline sells 5-wide at 9% credit-to-width with no pricing floor; the signal arm would sell 25-wide requiring ≥20%. A P&L difference between them would not isolate the sentiment gate.
Realised P&L exists, and one number in it is trustworthy. Four baseline closes, +$1.55 total, 4 of 4 winners. But positions 1–3 ran 7.7, 6.0 and 5.0 days against a 4-day rule, because they were unmarkable until 4 Aug and collected theta the rule does not grant. Position 4 closed on 7 Aug at exactly 4.0 days for +$0.30 — the first clean close, and the only honest per-position figure the project has.
A 4/4 win rate is what the structure should produce, not evidence. A 0.15-delta credit spread wins roughly 85% of the time by construction. This book wins often and loses large by design, so expectancy decides it, not win rate — and four positions in one rising, low-volatility regime is not a sample.
