Filters applied here

  • content_hash — SHA-256 over source+id+url+title, so the same story from one feed twice is stored once
  • has_substance — drops anything under 8 real words after stripping cashtags, @mentions and URLs. Kills $SPY $QQQ spam.
  • Full text pulled for news and macro links via trafilatura, 8 threads in parallel

The schema has no risk field. Risk is inferred from abs(direction) > 0.3 or magnitude > 0.5, so every prompt that improves direction trades away risk detection. The metric weighted 8× is measured through a proxy two other objectives fight over.
Model question settled. Sonnet 5 was graded on the same labels; its weighted error landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor. A prompt change (theta, 195 vs 233) beat a 4×-cost model upgrade.

Three things decide which 40 get sent

  • Source weight — macro 1.2, news/wire 1.0, social 0.6, plus a 1.3 ranking nudge for macro
  • Recency decay — weight halves every 72h, per item. Before this shipped, a 98-hour-old “U.S. and Iran halt attacks” outranked a 23-hour-old “attacks resume” on the same conflict.
  • Diversity cap — no source type may exceed 60% of the prompt

What the cap actually does

Macro sits at exactly 24 of 40 in every cycle since it shipped — the cap is saturated, not slack. Without it macro took 39 of 40.

But macro is the weakest source. Labels put macro at 24% relevant against news at 79%. The weights are inverted relative to measured value: the best source has the lowest weight. BBC World / Al Jazeera / NPR World are general news, not macro — every relevant macro item had a price channel.

Items with no title (retail chatter) never become stories. They collapse into one aggregate tone number, so 5,500 posts contribute a single figure.

Only this path produces the per-side tail estimates the trade gates depend on. The weighted-mean aggregator runs alongside it and supplies the sub-scores in each alert.

The tails are the useful output. 199 labels found the news corpus carries hazard, not direction — 188 of 199 items had no directional sign, but 20 flagged shock risk.

Four gates, in this order

1. confidence
< 0.65 → return [] — exits here every cycle. Confidence has never exceeded 0.42.

3. verticals
never reached
4. iron condor
never reached
The contradiction. The iron condor is the instrument for “no directional view, both tails quiet”. The code builds it. The confidence gate — which runs first — rejects the trade for having no directional view. The scanner declines condors on precisely the grounds that make a condor correct.
And the question is unanswerable. 188 of 199 labelled items carry no directional sign. The gate asks how sure the model is of a direction the inputs do not contain. No model fixes that — Sonnet was tried.

Fix: move the directional check so it filters verticals only, leaving condors to the tail gate, the edge threshold and the new premium-richness terms.

Exits

Close at 2× the credit received, or after 4 days, whichever comes first. Marked against the live chain every cycle.

The arms are not comparable. Baseline sells 5-wide at 9% credit-to-width with no pricing floor; the signal arm would sell 25-wide requiring ≥20%. A P&L difference between them would not isolate the sentiment gate.
