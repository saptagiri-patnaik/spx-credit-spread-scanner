Filters applied here

The schema now has a risk field. It was inferred from abs(direction) > 0.3 or magnitude > 0.5 — a proxy built from two fields answering different questions, so a shock of unclear direction scored zero on the metric weighted 8×. Asked directly since 4 Aug.
Model and prompt questions both settled. Sonnet 5 landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor, so the model stays Haiku. Re-graded with risk measured honestly, theta returns 30.0% risk recall against a 45% floor — so the prompt stays current too.
Read the numbers above as a new baseline, not an improvement. The grader changed and every variant's shared key block now asks for risk, which moved the other fields too. current's 233→177 is the measurement becoming honest, not the scorer getting better.
Still open: calibration. Stated confidence does not track accuracy, and item confidence is the weight in direction × confidence aggregation — so the corpus is weighted by a noisy number. theta was markedly better here (rising to 97.1%), which is the one reason to revisit it.

Three things decide which 40 get sent

  • Source weight — macro 1.2, news/wire 1.0, social 0.6, plus a 1.3 ranking nudge for macro
  • Recency decay — weight halves every 72h, per item. Before this shipped, a 98-hour-old “U.S. and Iran halt attacks” outranked a 23-hour-old “attacks resume” on the same conflict.
  • Diversity cap — no source type may exceed 60% of the prompt

What the cap actually does

Macro sits at exactly 24 of 40 in every cycle since it shipped — the cap is saturated, not slack. Without it macro took 39 of 40.

But macro is the weakest source. Labels put macro at 24% relevant against news at 79%. The weights are inverted relative to measured value: the best source has the lowest weight. BBC World / Al Jazeera / NPR World are general news, not macro — every relevant macro item had a price channel.

Items with no title (retail chatter) never become stories. They collapse into one aggregate tone number, so 5,500 posts contribute a single figure.

Reordered 4 August — tails first, direction scoped to the vertical

1. tails
_sides_allowed() — the real gate. 0 of 37 cycles blocked; both tails have stayed inside the 55% cap throughout.
2. build
verticals are always constructed, because a condor is assembled from one of each
3. offer verticals
only when confidence ≥ 0.40 — the directional trade, gated on a directional question
4. offer condor
whenever both tails are quiet, regardless of confidence
5. price
min_edge_score, POP, RoR, liquidity — now the binding constraints, and untested
The contradiction is resolved. The iron condor is the instrument for “no directional view, both tails quiet”, and the gate that ran first used to reject it for having no directional view. A vertical is short one tail and long a view, so uncertainty disqualifies it; a condor is short both tails and holds no view, so the same uncertainty is its precondition.
The unanswerable question is still unanswerable — it just no longer blocks everything. 188 of 199 labelled items carry no directional sign, so the gate asks how sure the model is of a direction the inputs do not contain. That is now scoped to the trade that actually needs a direction, rather than standing in front of the one that does not.
Not yet observed. All nine cycles of 4 August would have reached the condor path under this ordering, where none did before. Whether a condor then prices through the delta, POP, RoR and liquidity filters has never once been tested. First chance: 5 August, 06:55 PDT.

Fix: move the directional check so it filters verticals only, leaving condors to the tail gate, the edge threshold and the new premium-richness terms.

Exits

Close at 2× the credit received, or after 4 days, whichever comes first. Marked against a chain spanning the open expiries — its own fetch since 4 Aug, separate from the scan chain.

Marking was broken from the start, and silently. The scan chain spans dte_min..dte_max from today, so a position left it within days of opening and mark_spread() returned None. manage() skips an unmarkable position before testing the exits — so it was not merely unpriced, it was unclosable. The 4-day time exit never fired once. Fixed 4 Aug.
The arms are not comparable. Baseline sells 5-wide at 9% credit-to-width with no pricing floor; the signal arm would sell 25-wide requiring ≥20%. A P&L difference between them would not isolate the sentiment gate.
No realised P&L exists. Five baseline positions open, zero closed, in eight days. Three are past their hold and should close on the first in-session cycle after the fix — the first realised numbers this project will have produced.
