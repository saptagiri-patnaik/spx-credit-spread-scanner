The 09:03 edition listed three candidate explanations. One is now eliminated. “The corpus was too thin to support conviction” no longer holds: the 09:55 cycle ran on a properly diversified prompt — 24 macro, 15 news, 1 YouTube — and returned 34%, statistically indistinguishable from the lopsided cycles before it.

Two remain. Either the tape has genuinely been unattractive throughout the measurement window, or confidence_dir_scale compresses conviction so that 65% is unreachable in practice. The 09:55 rationale argues for the first: it calls the setup a “two-sided gap window rather than a clean premium-selling setup” and says “size down or stand aside”. That is a model declining to have a view, with reasons, rather than one failing to form one.

Answered, 2 August — and neither candidate was right. The session ran untouched and confidence moved (0.32–0.42, no two consecutive cycles alike), so the corpus was suppressing variance. But the level never approached the gate, and 199 hand labels explain why: 188 of 199 items carried no directional sign at all, and 70 of the 81 judged relevant were still zero. The gate asks how sure the model is of a direction that the inputs do not contain. No scorer and no model fixes that — Sonnet 5 was tried and its weighted error landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor.

Nextretire the gate rather than lower it. See the item below: it runs before the tail gate and makes the iron condor — the trade for exactly this situation — unreachable.

The confidence gate makes the iron condor unreachable

blocks 100% of cycles found 2 Aug

_candidates() checks directional confidence and returns an empty list before it ever consults _sides_allowed() or builds a condor:

Order in _candidates()GateReached?

The condor is the instrument for “no directional view, both tails quiet”. The code builds it; the gate above rejects the trade for having no directional view. The scanner declines condors on precisely the grounds that make a condor correct. _sides_allowed() already says so in its own docstring — “flat with both tails quiet is the best setup a premium seller gets” — so the reasoning was fixed one layer down and the gate above it was left alone. Sopana reached the same conclusion independently: when its directional votes fail to clear it sells a condor rather than standing aside.

The tail gate that should be deciding this has passed 18 of 18 cycles — downside 36–48%, upside 20–32%, against a 55% cap, zero breaches. Every prediction on record already says no adverse move is detected.

Actmove the directional check so it filters verticals only, leaving condors to the tail gate, min_edge_score and the new premium-richness terms. The close condition is simple and has never once been met: a cycle logging num_candidates > 0.

The scanner is dying mid-cycle on a native heap abort

3 events · 0 cycles lost blocked: instrumentation undeployed

Roughly one invocation in eighteen dies with glibc's double free or corruption and Runtime.ExitError. This is not a Python exception — the interpreter is killed outright, so the try/except in lambda_handler never runs and no traceback is written. It has been present since the Lambda cutover and went unnoticed because it always recovers: EventBridge invokes asynchronously, so Lambda retries the failed cycle by itself.

Event (UTC)glibc messageDied afterPeak memOutcome
30 Jul 00:21(!prev)6s396 MBretry also failed
31 Jul 13:10(out)9s517 MBrecovered on retry
31 Jul 19:55(!prev)8s439 MBrecovered on retry

Two explanations are already eliminated. It is not trafilatura's module-level HTML_PARSER being shared across the eight fulltext threads: lxml allocates a per-parser lock and acquires it in _ParserContext.prepare(), and its own docs call sharing “not harmful”. It is not cold starts either — 60 of 71 invocations are cold and only two of those died.

The leading suspect is psycopg2-binary, whose wheel bundles its own libssl-fe1b61af.so.3 and libcrypto-88208852.so.3 beside the OpenSSL that Python's own ssl module already loaded. Two OpenSSL copies in one process is a documented crash hazard, and it fits: the aborts land in the collection phase, where eight threads are doing concurrent HTTPS while libpq's copy is resident. Unproven — Docker Desktop was down, so the image itself was never opened.

The one anomaly that does not yet fit any theory: both 31 July aborts show higher peak memory than healthy cycles (517 and 439 MB against 330–390 MB) despite dying after eight seconds rather than running a full hundred. A cycle that dies that early should have allocated less, not more.

Act ifa retry fails too — spx-scanner-errors was retuned to ≥2 errors in 15 min on 31 Jul and now means exactly that: a cycle genuinely lost. Blocked meanwhile on shipping faulthandler.enable(all_threads=True), which is written but uncommitted; until it deploys every abort stays silent. Once it lands, the dumped thread stack names the library — if it is not psycopg2 or ssl, the OpenSSL theory is dead.

The synthesis path weighted an item the same on day 7 of the lookback as in its first hour, while the mean aggregator had decayed items all along. That is why the five cycles above are identical: the model was handed a near-identical prompt every 45 minutes and, reasonably, returned a near-identical answer. Those are not five pieces of evidence that the tape is unattractive — they are one, sampled five times. Per-item scoring model, Haiku against the alternatives

The eval set is no longer the blocker: 199 items hand-labelled on 1 Aug against label_relevant / label_direction / label_risk. Four graded runs since. Rank on weighted error — it is the only metric that prices a missed shock (×8) against a false alarm (×1).

Re-running current unchanged gave 233 against 236, so run-to-run noise is ~1.3%. That is the yardstick everything else is read against: Sonnet 5's 231 sits inside the noise floor, while theta's 195 is a real 16% gain. A free prompt change beat a 4×-cost model upgrade by an order of magnitude. The model question is closed — stay on Haiku.

theta is not yet safe to ship. Its gain comes entirely from cutting false alarms (82→36) and wrong directions (63→31) by being far more conservative — it flags 72 items where current flags 153. That same conservatism drove risk recall down 45%→20%, so 128 of its remaining 195 error (66%) is now missed risk: the one failure that costs real money. It also skews bear:bull 5.0:1 against a labelled 1.75:1, which would feed straight into the per-side tail gate and bias against put spreads.

The deeper finding is that no prompt can fix this. Risk recall reads 45 / 45 / 20 / 15 across the four runs because SCORE_SCHEMA has no risk field — the grader infers it from abs(direction) > 0.3 or magnitude > 0.5, so every prompt that improves direction trades away risk detection. The most heavily weighted metric is measured through a proxy two other objectives are fighting over. A prediction that theta's “high magnitude even at direction 0” line would open the second path was tested and falsified — risk recall fell rather than rose.

Switch whenall four hold on a re-grade against the same 199 labels: (1) item_scores records the prompt variant, not just the model — today save_score() stores model only, so a production A/B would be unattributable; (2) an explicit risk field exists in SCORE_SCHEMA and is scored directly rather than inferred; (3) weighted error below 233 — current's measured baseline, not 236; (4) risk recall at or above 45%, i.e. no regression on the expensive metric, and bear:bull inside ~2.5:1. Fail any one and stay on current.
Timeline4 Aug — ship prompt attribution + the risk field, re-grade (~15 min, ~$0.35), decide against the four gates above. If they pass, switch SCORING_PROMPT the same day. Effect is not immediate: recent_scores() reads a 7-day lookback, so synthesis sees a mix of old- and new-prompt scores until ~11 Aug. Judge nothing before then. First clean read: 11 Aug.
