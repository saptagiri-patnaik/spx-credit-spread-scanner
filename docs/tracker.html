The 09:03 edition listed three candidate explanations. One is now eliminated. “The corpus was too thin to support conviction” no longer holds: the 09:55 cycle ran on a properly diversified prompt — 24 macro, 15 news, 1 YouTube — and returned 34%, statistically indistinguishable from the lopsided cycles before it.

Two remain. Either the tape has genuinely been unattractive throughout the measurement window, or confidence_dir_scale compresses conviction so that 65% is unreachable in practice. The 09:55 rationale argues for the first: it calls the setup a “two-sided gap window rather than a clean premium-selling setup” and says “size down or stand aside”. That is a model declining to have a view, with reasons, rather than one failing to form one.

Answered, 2 August — and neither candidate was right. The session ran untouched and confidence moved (0.32–0.42, no two consecutive cycles alike), so the corpus was suppressing variance. The gate asks how sure the model is of a direction that the inputs do not contain. No scorer and no model fixes that — Sonnet 5 was tried and its weighted error landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor.

4 August — a third explanation, and this one is measurable. The gate is a fixed threshold on a number whose scale changed underneath it. Split the 180 predictions on the day the synthesis aggregator replaced the weighted mean and they are plainly two different distributions:

When is this stable enough to wire into Sopana?

added 9 Aug — the question the open items keep obscuring

Two different stability questions, and only one of them is on the critical path

Every item on this page is about trade construction — delta caps, RoR floors, POP, premium richness, edge thresholds. None of that is what Sopana consumes. The joint roadmap defines the handoff as a veto only: block the put side when downside risk is fat, block entries when a high-impact print lands inside the holding window. It may never open a trade Sopana would not have opened anyway.

So the scanner has two readiness bars, and conflating them is why this feels like it never stabilises — every fix reveals the next binding constraint in a funnel that Sopana does not read.

 Track A — the veto Sopana consumesTrack B — the signal arm
Output downside_risk, upside_risk, event_risk a ranked, priced, tradeable spread

Ready when ~19 Aug on the current clock; veto shadow-logs from 21 Aug not date-bound. Needs IV/RV ≥ 1 and a measured POP.

Track A does not wait on Track B, and never did. The signal arm having opened zero positions is not evidence against the veto — the veto subtracts trades from Sopana, so an arm that declines to trade and a veto that blocks trades are the same disposition expressed twice. The worst case of a wrong veto is fewer Sopana trades, not a new failure mode, which is exactly why it goes first and in shadow.

Stable meansfor Track A: (1) Gate 8 closes at 10 clean trading days; (2) the tail estimates have been observed firing, not merely passing — they are 0-for-52 on blocking either side, so a gate that has never said no is unvalidated in the only direction that matters; (3) event_risk is exported on a window matching the consumer's holding period, not the scanner's. See the new item below. None of these require a single signal trade.

premium_weight is 0.15 because it was typed, not because it was measured

measured 9 Aug · instrument shipped found 7 Aug · measured 9 Aug

On 7 August premium_edge cost 0.038 of a 0.05 gate — three quarters of the bar on its own. Remove that one term and all nine candidates clear. It therefore decides whether the signal arm trades in a cheap-premium regime, which is more authority than an unvalidated constant should have.

The question was posed as “is 0.15 too harsh”. It is too lenient. The term proxies for something computable: pop = 1 - short_delta, and delta is risk-neutral, so when the index moves more than the options price the real breach chance is higher than delta says. Reprice POP on realised vol, difference the expected values, and the weight that reproduces the correction falls out.

MeasureValueReads as
implied weight, median of 25 suggestions0.235mean 0.223, range 0.148–0.278
weight applied0.150under-penalising by ~1.5×
correction the arithmetic wants−0.052vs −0.034 applied
suggestions that are positive-EV on honest POP2 of 250 of 9 on 7 Aug, at any drift

That last row is the uncomfortable one. Nearly every candidate the scanner has ever produced loses money once POP stops being borrowed from the option's own price. On this reading min_edge_score has been refusing them correctly, and the signal arm's silence is the best-calibrated output the system currently has.

Two biases, pulling opposite ways — which is why the fix is a repricing and not a bigger constant. Delta is N(d1) where the probability is N(d2), worth +0.010 and present in every regime, modelled nowhere. The risk-neutral premium is worth −0.068 on 7 August's tape. Net −0.058 against the −0.038 the weight applies.

And no constant is right, because the shape is wrong. Fitted across a grid of forward-vol windows × drift assumptions the implied weight ranges 0.17 to 1.84 and changes sign, because w · (IV/RV − 1) vanishes as the ratio approaches 1 while the true correction does not — it depends on the candidate's own strike and DTE, which one flat ATM ratio cannot see. Exactly one cell defends 0.15: RV30 with zero drift, at 0.167. The scanner feeds the ratio from RV20.

Shipped, deliberately inertr72 records pop_real and premium_edge_measured per candidate and scores neither — edge is byte-for-byte unchanged. Folding the measurement in would have changed behaviour on five days of IV/RV history and left nothing to compare against, which is how confidence_gate closed for a fortnight. Revisit when the recorded series can say whether the gap is the regime or the formula. Do not raise the weight to 0.235 in the meantime.

The event-risk window is right for the scanner and wrong for Sopana's veto

shipped 19 Aug · producer only found 9 Aug · shipped 19 Aug

Shipped the producer side. Every prediction's market_context.hazard now carries the event horizon alongside the unchanged event_risk flag: next_high_impact_at and days_until_next_high_impact, computed from the same dte_max-windowed event query the flag already used (25 days today, which happens to equal Sopana's own DTE, so nothing needed widening). The same block also names raw vs. effective downside/upside risk explicitly (*_raw from the calibration stamp, *_effective from what gating actually reads — identical numbers when calibration hasn't run, which is correct, not a gap) plus as_of, calibration_mode and build_version, so a consumer reading this block never has to also join the predictions row for its own recency check. event_risk and the gating fields are untouched byte-for-byte — this is purely additive. The original finding, kept because it is what the fix was aimed at:

r70 narrowed event_risk from “a high-impact release anywhere in dte_max” (25 days, therefore always true — 190 of 190) to event_risk_window_days = 4, chosen to equal paper_hold_days: a release the position will actually sit through. That is correct for the scanner, whose paper positions close at 4 days.

The joint roadmap specifies the Sopana veto as “block entries when a high-impact print lands inside the DTE window”. Sopana is a 25-DTE strategy. A 4-day flag cannot answer a 25-day question — it will read false for releases 5 to 25 days out that a Sopana position sits through in full. Wire the flag across as-is and the veto silently under-fires on precisely the events it exists to catch.

This is the same defect as the one r70 fixed, mirrored: a window hard-coded to one consumer's holding period and then read by another. The fix is not to widen it back — that would restore the always-true behaviour for the scanner — but to stop exporting a boolean at all.

What remains is entirely on Sopana's side, outside this workspace: read market_context.hazard.next_high_impact_at / days_until_next_high_impact and apply its own 25-day window instead of this scanner's 4-day event_risk flag.

At the same delta the call collects 57% more premium. That confirms the mechanism the 5 August item suspected, and which is already written as a comment above the ror_floor reject: put skew flattens the put price curve in the OTM region, so a fixed-width put spread collects less per point of width than the same-width call spread. Under the event-risk cap the best reachable put RoR is 0.163 against a 0.20 floor — not close. Lift the cap and it reaches 0.235 and clears.

The consequence is the one worth stating plainly, because it undercuts the experiment the whole system exists to run: the scanner has produced call credit spreads and nothing else for its entire recorded history — 11 of 11 stored suggestions. A signal arm that can only ever express itself as one structure is not testing the signal.

Corrected, same day, before anything was changed. This item first proposed making the floor side-aware or letting the put side flex to wider structures. Both would have been wrong, and the reason is that the scanner already contains a second, better theory of when a spread is worth taking — the one it ranks on:

Not one put pairing on the entire chain has positive expected value — 0 of 512, best ev_ratio −0.039. The eight that scrape past the flat floor are all negative-EV. Widening makes it worse, not better: put RoR falls with width (0.205 at 5-wide to 0.185 at 50) while the break-even rises. So the floor is rejecting the put side correctly. Lowering it, or splitting it by side, would have admitted nothing but losers — a threshold change that looks like it fixes the symptom and buys a book of negative-EV trades.

The total-loss assumption in ev_ratio was checked before trusting it, since charging a full max_loss on every breach would bias exactly this comparison. It holds: median P(full loss | short breached) is 0.98 at 5-wide and 0.82 even at 50-wide, and a partial-loss refinement moves the best candidate's EV by +0.0035. Not the explanation.

What is actually defective is the floor's shape. A flat 0.20 and the ranker's d/(1−d) agree at exactly one delta — 0.167 — and diverge everywhere else, lenient above it and strict below. On the call side today that costs both ways: the floor admits 41 pairings the ranker scores negative and rejects 32 it scores positive.

POP is a risk-neutral probability, which may make put spreads unsellable by construction

promoted 9 Aug · now instrumented found 6 Aug

The item above concludes the put side is genuinely unattractive. That conclusion is only as good as the probability it rests on, and the probability is borrowed from the option's own price: pop = 1 - short_delta.

Delta is a risk-neutral quantity. It already contains the variance and skew risk premium — the very thing a premium seller is trying to harvest, and the reason OTM puts trade above their historical frequency of paying out. Using it as the probability of success means the model evaluates put spreads with the seller's edge already subtracted. The steeper the skew, the more it subtracts, and the skew is always steepest on the downside. A model built this way cannot find a put spread attractive, regardless of whether one is.

This does not overturn the finding above — the put/call gap is large and consistent, and the current regime is genuinely poor for selling (IV/RV 0.78–0.83, options pricing less movement than the index has delivered). But it does mean the put side has never been evaluated on a real-world probability, only on the market's own.

9 August — this stopped being a hypothesis about the put side and became the central measurement. It is the same defect the premium-weight item measures from the other direction, and r72 now records the correction on every candidate as pop_real. Two findings promote it out of the watch list:

It costs far more than this item guessed, and it is not put-specific: on 7 August the correction ran −0.050 to −0.068 on call spreads, moving POP from 0.78–0.83 down to 0.73–0.79. And it contains a second bias nobody had named — pop = 1 - short_delta reads N(d1) as a probability when the probability is N(d2), worth +0.010 in the other direction and present in every regime, rich or cheap.

The put-specific claim in this item therefore stands but is no longer the interesting part. The scanner does not merely evaluate puts with the seller's edge subtracted — it evaluates everything that way, and on 7 August's tape that was the difference between nine tradeable candidates and none.

Testtwo now run in parallel. (1) The breach test, unchanged and still needing months: per side, realised breach rate against delta-implied. (2) The recorded series from r72, which needs weeks not months — if premium_edge_measured stays ~1.5× the applied term across regimes, the formula is wrong; if it converges as IV/RV crosses 1, the regime was. Nothing changes POP until one of them reports, because POP also feeds min_pop (0.68) and moving it silently re-scales a second threshold — the event_risk trap again. Blast radius checked: 7 Aug's real-world POPs land 0.73–0.79, all still clear 0.68.

The confidence gate makes the iron condor unreachable

fixed 4 Aug found 2 Aug · fixed 4 Aug

Fixed, and by reordering rather than by lowering. The earlier recalibration to 0.40 only changed how often the wrong question got asked. The gate now scopes to the directional trade: verticals are still constructed unconditionally, because a condor is assembled from one of each, but they are only offered when confidence clears. The condor no longer passes through the directional check at all — it is decided by _sides_allowed(), min_edge_score and the pricing filters, none of which ask which way the market is going.

Premium richness shipped in the same change, as an edge input rather than the second veto it was tempting to make it. IV/RV below 1 lowers the score; it does not refuse the trade. At weight 0.15 today's 0.85–0.90 costs ~0.02 of edge against a 0.05 threshold, and the ratio is clamped to 0.5–1.5 so one bad quote cannot own the ranking. The discipline is the lesson from this very item: a hard floor on a number with days of history is how the confidence gate came to block every cycle for a fortnight.

The container deploy path cannot ship from this machine

routed around 3 Aug container path still dead

Twenty-one push attempts on 3 Aug — two deploy.ps1 runs and two direct docker push loops — all died against Docker Desktop's proxy at 192.168.65.1:3128, with broken pipe or use of closed network connection. Not credentials: re-authenticating changed nothing, and the script's “ECR login expires” hint is misleading here.

It is purely blob size. Splitting the dependency install from three layers to six pushed the 67.5 MB layer through on the next attempt. What remains is ADD file:41dd4d7f… at 433 MB — the root filesystem of public.ecr.aws/lambda/python:3.12. That is AWS's layer; it cannot be split from this side.

Resolved by removing the upload, not by fixing it. A ZIP package carries no base layer — AWS supplies the runtime — so the payload is 32.6 MB against 433 MB, under the 50 MB direct-upload limit and needing no S3 hop. The Dockerfile justified the image because dependencies "exceed Lambda's 250 MB unzipped limit"; measured, the payload is 95.6 MB. That premise was wrong.

The lesson, which is the part worth keeping. The 250 MB claim was never tested. The only figure that ever looked large was the 185 MB site-packages reading — and that was taken inside the container, so it counted AWS's 49 MB as if it were ours. A measurement taken inside the thing being justified will tend to justify it. The container was adopted to escape a limit it was never near, and in exchange it carried a 433 MB upload this network cannot deliver — which cost a day and held three commits out of production.

There was no proxy to bypass, contrary to the first diagnosis: HTTP_PROXY unset, no proxies in ~/.docker/config.json, no proxy keys in Docker Desktop's settings, 3128 absent from all of them. 192.168.65.1:3128 is Docker's own transparent gateway, exactly as the Dockerfile comment said.

GitHub Actions was proposed here and deferred. It was raised while the container push was failing, on the theory that building from a hosted runner would sidestep this machine's upload. It would have — but the ZIP removed the 433 MB upload altogether, so the problem CI was going to solve no longer exists. A deploy is now ./deploy/build-zip.ps1 -Deploy, about three minutes.

Building it now would add a second deploy path, a stored AWS credential and a release surface nobody watches, in exchange for saving three minutes on a deploy that happens roughly weekly. The version worth having later is CI, not CD: run the 217 tests on every push, and leave the deploy manual. That is the half that catches something — the tests currently run only when someone remembers.

Standingthe container path is still dead and is no longer used. deploy/build-zip.ps1 verifies rather than trusts: it aborts if any .pyd survives, if no .so exists, or if psycopg2/lxml are missing — a Windows wheel would otherwise import-fail on the first cycle rather than at build time.
