Where it stands

11 Aug 2026

The pipeline is a funnel that has always ended in zero. The stage doing the stopping has moved four times in six days, and on 9 August the reason changed character entirely: for the first time the answer is not “a filter is miscalibrated” but “the trades are bad and the system was right to refuse them”.

Read this section first if you are trying to work out whether the scanner is finished. It has two outputs and they are nowhere near the same maturity. The tail estimates and the event-risk flag — the only things Sopana consumes, and it consumes them as a veto — depend on the scorer, synthesis and uptime, and are gated on Gate 8's clean-day clock, currently 2 of 10 and closing ~19 Aug. The tradeable spread depends on every pricing filter in this log and is not date-bound at all.

Everything below that reads like an unending sequence of defects belongs to the second output. The first has been quietly working since 29 July. The handoff was never waiting on the signal arm, and the operational tracker now carries the full criteria.

Every blocker removed on 4 August was upstream of the decision, not the decision itself. The confidence gate, the marking chain and the risk proxy were all instrumentation defects: three separate ways the system could not observe what it was doing. Removing them does not make the strategy work — it makes the strategy testable for the first time.

What is left is the strategy question, untested. Whether a condor prices through the delta, POP, RoR and liquidity filters has never once been observed. 5 August is the first session in which any of this can be watched rather than reasoned about.

5 Aug, midday — the session answered a different question. It was not the strategy that got observed but the write path: every cycle that produced a candidate died saving it, and the first two and a half hours of the session recorded nothing. Once fixed, one cycle did reach the filters and declined on them — best edge 0.04 against the 0.05 threshold. So the sentence above is now true, roughly three hours later than it claimed, and the funnel still ends in zero for a reason nobody has tested yet.

5 Aug, after the close — the reason is now half-known. Seven candidates across the session, every edge between 0.012 and 0.040, none clearing 0.05. The shortfall is largely one measured input: premium_edge ran −0.028 to −0.033 all day on IV/RV of 0.78–0.83. The funnel ends in zero because premium was cheap, not because a threshold is miscalibrated — the first time that sentence has been supported by a number rather than asserted.

A second thing surfaced that nobody was looking for: every candidate all day was a call spread, against a model that said UP eight times. Both tails were open and the ranking favoured puts, so the put side is being filtered out before it can compete. That is an open question, not a finding, and r67 ships the counters to settle it.

11 Aug — the scanner can now be wrong on the record. Until this week nothing stored whether a prediction was right; the data existed but only a report ever read it. prediction_outcomes settles each matured prediction against what followed, and a correction is refitted from that series every cycle.

The first thing it learned was to change nothing, and that is the finding. Zero downside breaches in 43 windows against a stated 25–42% looks like a ten-fold overstatement until the windows are counted honestly — 45 minutes apart across 14 observed days, they deflate to about three independent observations of one calm, rising, contango regime, and the upper bound on the true breach rate is still 0.79. The stated value sits inside that interval, so it stands. The number to read on this layer is n_eff, never the row count, and it is currently 1.0 against a floor of 8.

The one durable finding, and it is not the one the project was built on. 199 hand-labelled items say the news corpus carries hazard, not direction: 188 of 199 had no directional sign at all, and 70 of the 81 judged relevant were still zero — but 20 flagged shock risk. The per-side tail estimates, not the direction number, are the scanner’s product. Everything downstream has been reshaped around that.

The experiment: the dumb rule against the model

standings as of 7 Aug 2026, close

The whole project reduces to one question, and it is not “did the model make money”. It is did reading the news beat selling premium without reading it. Two arms run side by side in the same simulator, against the same chain, with the same exits, so the difference between them is the model and nothing else.

After ten trading days there is a control group and no treatment group. The baseline has opened eight positions. The signal arm has opened zero, all time — it has never once cleared all three gates. The experiment cannot answer its question yet, and every additional day accumulates evidence only for the arm that ignores the model.

This is not a malfunction. Refusing to trade is a designed outcome. But “the gate is correctly conservative” and “the gate is set where nothing can pass” look identical from outside, and nothing currently distinguishes them.

9 Aug — something finally distinguishes them. Repricing POP on realised vol makes 23 of the 25 candidates the scanner has ever produced negative-EV, and 0 of 9 on 7 August at any drift assumption. That is the first evidence that the gate is conservative correctly rather than arbitrarily, and it arrives from a direction nobody was looking: not by loosening anything, but by checking the probability the whole ranking rests on. The measurement is recorded and not yet acted on.

The +$1.25 is flattered, and by a known amount of extra time. All three closures fired on the time rule, but none was held for four days: positions 1, 2 and 3 ran 7.7, 6.0 and 5.0 days. They became unmarkable on 31 July when the scan window slid past their expiries, and could not close until the marking chain was fixed on 4 August.

A credit spread held past its intended exit collects more theta, so the control arm’s only three data points each earned 1–3.7 extra days of decay that the rule does not grant. Treat +$1.25 as an upper bound on the dumb rule, not a measurement of it. Positions 4, 5 and 6 are the first that will close on the rule as written.

Also worth holding lightly: six positions is not a sample, all six are the same structure at the same delta in a single rising, low-volatility regime, and a 3/3 win rate is exactly what a 0.15-delta credit spread should produce — roughly 85% of them win by construction. The number that decides this is expectancy, not win rate, because this book wins often and loses large by design.

What would settle it. The signal arm needs to open positions at all, which needs an edge above 0.05 — and 5 August showed the shortfall is mostly premium_edge tracking a cheap-options regime. So the first honest read comes when IV/RV crosses 1.0 and stays there for a session or two. Until then the standing instruction holds: do not lower min_edge_score to manufacture a treatment group. An arm that fires because its bar was moved measures the bar, not the model.

Reproduce these numbers with python -m tools.paper_report, which splits both arms and reports expectancy, win rate and average win against average loss.

Phase 2

The measurement scaffolding — 28 July

One heavy day, thirteen commits. Everything that makes the scanner gradeable rather than merely running landed here — and so did the tail gate, whose reasoning took another week to reach the gate sitting above it.

28 Jul Item-quality filter, prompt registry, and a labelled eval harness

has_substance drops anything under eight real words once cashtags, @mentions and URLs are stripped, which kills $SPY $QQQ spam before it costs a scoring call. The prompt registry makes variants nameable; the harness makes them comparable.

28 Jul Claude scoring backend behind the existing client interface

One call per item, chunked at 6,000 characters, returning strict JSON. The scorer that produced each score is recorded from the start — the prompt that produced it was not, and that gap cost a week in August.

28 Jul Tier-2 synthesis aggregator, alongside the weighted mean

One Opus call over the top stories produces the view; the weighted mean stays as the source of the sub-scores in each alert. Rationale formatting hardened, and the synthesis model’s answer surfaced verbatim rather than paraphrased.

This is the change whose scale shift silently closed the confidence gate. Nothing re-derived the threshold when the aggregator underneath it was replaced.

28 Jul Untitled social chatter kept out of story clustering

Retail posts with no title never become stories — they collapse into a single aggregate tone number, so ~5,500 posts contribute one figure instead of flooding the prompt.

28 Jul Paper trading: two arms, a stop, and calibration metrics

Simulated positions with a 2×-credit stop and a 4-day time exit. A baseline arm sells a ~0.15-delta put spread daily, ignoring the model entirely, as the control the signal arm is measured against. Scoring runs every cycle; market-hours mode defers only the prediction.

28 Jul Per-side tail-risk gate and iron condors

_sides_allowed() asks whether either tail is fat, and the condor is added as the trade for “no view, both tails quiet”. Its docstring recorded the argument at the time: “Blocking on a NEUTRAL label asked the wrong question — flat with both tails quiet is the best setup a premium seller gets.”

The reasoning was correct and it was fixed one layer down. The confidence gate above it was left alone, which is the defect that took until 4 August to find.

28 Jul Trade signals routed to their own webhook; repeat alerts stopped
28 Jul First Lambda deployment: handler, container image, runbook

The container choice is the one that had to be undone on 3 August — recorded in the tracker at the time as chosen for a limit the function was never near.

Deployment scripted and driven from VS Code, runbook rewritten around it. A version is stamped on every log line and every alert, and the schedule anchored so a build is identifiable from its output alone. Logs and alerts render in Pacific with AWS owning the DST transition.

Phase 4

Instrumenting what the strategy actually turns on — 2 August

2 Aug Volatility regime measured for the first time

Nothing had measured the two questions a theta strategy turns on. Four measures now do, all arithmetic on data already fetched:

  • IV / RV — ATM implied against 20-day realised. Above 1, the market charges more for a move than the index has delivered.
  • VIX / VIX3M — above 1 is backwardation, historically a poor regime for selling premium.
  • IV rank — where today sits in its own trailing range. Needs ~20 daily readings.
  • trend_score — 5-day move, saturating at 3%, used to pick a side, never to predict.

trend_side_block ships at 0, deliberately. It is the only one of the four that changes which trades may exist, and landing a behaviour change in the same deploy as the instrumentation meant to judge it would leave no clean before-picture.

2 Aug 199 hand labels graded — the corpus carries hazard, not direction

188 of 199 items carried no directional sign at all, and 70 of the 81 judged relevant were still zero — but 20 flagged shock risk. The confidence gate asks how sure the model is of a direction the inputs do not contain. No scorer and no model fixes that.

Tested rather than assumed: Sonnet 5 was run against Haiku and its weighted error landed inside the 1.3% run-to-run noise floor. The model stays claude-haiku-4-5.

2 Aug The condor contradiction found

_candidates() checks directional confidence and returns an empty list before it ever consults _sides_allowed() or builds a condor. A vertical is short one tail and long a view, so “not sure which way” disqualifies it; a condor is short both tails and holds no view, so the same uncertainty is its precondition.

The scanner was declining condors on exactly the grounds that make a condor correct. Fixed 4 August, by reordering rather than by lowering.

2 Aug Diagnostics: a thread stack when the interpreter dies, and an alarm that means something

faulthandler prints a full thread dump on a native death, not just a glibc line. The abort alarm was rescoped to fire when a cycle is actually lost rather than when one abort fires, since EventBridge retries asynchronously and most aborts recover.

Phase 5

The deploy path and the heap abort — 3 August

3 Aug Native heap abort diagnosed and fixedfixed

Roughly one invocation in eighteen died with glibc’s double free or corruption and Runtime.ExitError. Not a Python exception — the interpreter is killed outright, so lambda_handler’s try/except never ran and no traceback was written. It had been present since the Lambda cutover and went unnoticed because the async retry always absorbed it.

The dump deployed the day before caught the fourth abort and named it: all eight fulltext workers inside trafilatura, three of them in htmlprocessing.prune_unwanted_nodes at the same instant, message corrupted double-linked list.

The leading suspect was wrong. psycopg2-binary’s bundled OpenSSL was loaded — it appears in the extension-module list — but no thread was executing in it. The earlier elimination of the shared parser had checked the right lock and drawn a conclusion about the wrong operation: lxml does lock parsing, but prune_unwanted_nodes mutates the tree afterwards, outside the lock, and all eight threads share trafilatura’s one module-level HTML_PARSER and so libxml2’s interned string dictionary.

Fixed with a module-level lock around trafilatura.extract(). The HTTP fetch above it stays parallel — a 15 s timeout per article against tens of milliseconds of parsing — so throughput is essentially unchanged. Five tests pin it: extraction never overlaps, fetching still does, a failing document releases the lock.

Closes whenten consecutive trading days complete with no lost cycle. The lock reached production 3 Aug 18:55 PT, so the count starts 4 Aug. It stays a gate because a fix nobody has watched run is a hypothesis.
3 Aug Deploy path rebuilt — ZIP instead of containerclosed

The container push had failed roughly thirty times on its 433 MB base layer and was holding three commits out of production. A ZIP uploads 32.6 MB instead, because AWS supplies the runtime. New function spx-scanner-zip: same role, memory, timeout and all 76 environment variables. The three spx-scanner-grid-* schedules and both alarms now point at it; spx-scanner stays in place, unscheduled, as rollback.

Two traps caught on the way. spx-scheduler-role could invoke only the old function, so repointing without widening it would have failed every firing silently. And scheduler update-schedule replaces the whole definition, so each schedule’s cron, timezone and one-attempt retry policy had to be echoed back rather than defaulted away.

Closed 3 Auga scheduled firing landed on spx-scanner-zip at 01:55 UTC running r50-gf13dd1c, with zero invocations on the old function since the cutover. Deploys are now ./deploy/build-zip.ps1 -Deploy, about three minutes.
3 Aug Decided: premium richness is an edge input, never a hard gate

IV/RV folds into min_edge_score so thin premium lowers the score rather than vetoing the trade. It does not get its own threshold.

The reason is not hypothetical — it is the confidence gate. confidence_gate = 0.65 was a single unvalidated number placed in front of everything, and it blocked 100% of cycles for a fortnight while nobody could tell whether the bar or the signal was wrong. There were four days of IV/RV history. A hard floor on that repeats the mistake with a fresher number: an arm that never fires, for a new reason.

Revisit when~40 trading days of IV/RV exist and the distribution is known. A hard floor is defensible against a measured distribution; it is not defensible against four days.
3 Aug First full live session under the ZIP, and the first regime readings

Ten market-hours cycles, a Regime: line on every one, and the abort diagnostic printed a full thread dump — naming the cause rather than merely capturing it.

  • IV / RV 0.84–0.93 — below 1 all day: options priced less movement than SPX delivered. That is the seller’s edge running backwards, and there was no way to see it before 2 August.
  • VIX / VIX3M 0.829–0.850 — contango, the normal seller-friendly regime.
  • IV rank 1 / 20 — first reading banked, iv 0.1085.
  • trend_score +0.35 — mild uptrend, inside the (disabled) block threshold.

ATM IV sitting below VIX is the cross-check that the calculation is right: VIX prices the whole skew including out-of-the-money puts, so an at-the-money strike should read lower.

Phase 6

Four fixes and a falsified hypothesis — 4 August

The day the scanner stopped being blocked by its own instrumentation. Two of the three things keeping it from producing a single measurable trade were removed; the third turned out not to be a measurement problem at all.

4 Aug Confidence gate recalibrated, 0.65 → 0.40shipped

The gate was a fixed threshold on a number whose scale changed underneath it. Split the 180 predictions on the day synthesis replaced the weighted mean and they are plainly two distributions:

0.65 was already a near-dead threshold under the mean aggregator, which is why no signal trade had ever opened even before the swap. Synthesis then reported genuinely differentiated but systematically lower conviction and the gate became unreachable outright. Nothing alarmed: a gate that blocks every cycle looks identical in the logs to a market that never qualifies.

0.40 is the synthesis 75th percentile, so the gate keeps its intended meaning — trade the upper quartile of conviction — on the scale in use. Against the 41 cycles on record it admits 15; against 4 August’s session, 5 of 9. Provisional, and deliberately so: 41 observations from seven sessions of one calm, contango, IV-under-RV regime.

4 Aug Directional gate rescoped to verticals — the condor is decided on its own meritsshipped

The recalibration alone only changed how often the wrong question got asked. Verticals are still constructed unconditionally, because a condor is assembled from one of each, but they are only offered when confidence clears. The condor no longer passes through the directional check at all — it is decided by _sides_allowed(), min_edge_score and the pricing filters, none of which ask which way the market is going.

The gate order now reads: tails first, build both verticals, offer verticals only above 0.40, offer the condor whenever both tails are quiet regardless of confidence, then price it.

Checka cycle logs num_candidates > 0 with confidence below the directional gate. All nine cycles of 4 Aug would now reach that path, where none did before — both tails sat 22–40% against the 55% cap while confidence never cleared 0.42.
4 Aug Premium richness wired into the edge score at weight 0.15shipped

IV/RV below 1 lowers min_edge_score rather than vetoing the trade, so an unusually good structure can still sell into thin premium. Today’s 0.85–0.90 ratio costs ~0.02 of edge against a 0.05 threshold — enough to demand a better structure, not enough to refuse one. The ratio is clamped to 0.5–1.5 first, so a bad quote tilts the ranking rather than owning it.

PREMIUM_WEIGHT is exposed in .env, with the other tunable thresholds.

4 Aug Marking given its own chain, so positions can closeshipped

The chain was fetched once per cycle over dte_min..dte_max measured from today — a 20-to-25-day window sliding forward daily. A position opened inside that window falls out of it within days, so its legs are absent from every later chain and mark_spread() returns None.

The severe part is second-order. manage() hits continue on an unmarkable position before it evaluates the exits, so such a position is not merely unpriced — it is unclosable. The 4-day time exit never fired, the stop never fired, and paper_positions held zero closed rows and zero realised P&L across eight days of trading, with three positions sitting past their hold.

4 Aug Risk asked directly, and prompt attribution added to every scoreshipped

SCORE_SCHEMA had no risk field. The metric weighted ×8 in the grader was inferred from abs(direction) > 0.3 or magnitude > 0.5 — a proxy built from two fields answering different questions, so a shock of unclear direction scored zero on the thing that matters most.

Asked directly, current returns 70.0% risk recall, missing 6 of 20 shock items. Per-chunk combination takes the max rather than the confidence-weighted mean: one paragraph naming a shock makes the article risky, and averaging dilutes the warning.

item_scores now records the prompt variant that actually ran, so a production A/B is attributable instead of a week of mixed corpus nobody can separate.

4 Aug Prompt question settled — stay on current, and the hypothesis behind the work is falsified

Re-graded on the same 199 labels with risk measured rather than inferred, theta returns 30.0% risk recall against the 45% floor, missing 14 of 20 labelled shocks. Its weighted-error edge over current is 168 to 177 — nine points, bought with a 40-point deficit on the metric priced at ×8. Gates: weighted error ✓, bear:bull 1.6:1 ✓, risk recall ✗. Fail one and stay. So, stay.

The falsified claim is the most useful result of the day. The roadmap held that the proxy was why theta scored badly on risk — that any prompt improving direction must trade away a risk signal being read off direction. Asked the question directly, theta still reaches only 30%. Its conservatism suppresses risk detection genuinely. The proxy was a real defect and worth removing; it was not theta’s excuse, and removing it did not rescue the variant it was blamed for.

Read carefullythe re-graded numbers are not comparable to the earlier four runs. The grader changed and every variant’s shared key block now asks for risk, which moved the other fields too. current’s 233→177 is the measurement becoming honest, not the scorer improving. Only current vs theta under today’s identical conditions carries the decision.
4 Aug API request timeouts set: 30 s scoring, 120 s synthesisshipped

The 15:55 PDT cycle ran the full 600 s and returned Status: timeout having logged only collection. The automatic retry, on identical code and environment, finished in 127 s. The cause was a default nobody set: the SDK read timeout of 600 s is identical to the Lambda budget, so one hung request can outlive the cycle and nothing degrades — it just dies. A typical Haiku scoring call takes 2–5 s.

Separated rather than shared because synthesis is one Opus call over 40 stories at ~15 s, and putting it on the scorer’s budget would cut off the cycle’s single most valuable request. The SDK’s own two retries still fit inside a cycle.

Worth stating plainly: this was not caused by the deploy it followed. It landed one minute after r55 went live — exactly the coincidence that invites a wrong conclusion — but r55 changed the gate value, the marking chain and two deploy scripts, none of which the scoring path touches, and the retry succeeded on that same code.

Watcha second timeout would falsify the diagnosis, since a per-request cap now bounds it. Cycle duration should stay in the 110–160 s band it has held all week.
4 Aug sync-env.ps1 was writing settings to the retired functionpartly open

The script pushed .env to $FuncName, which common.ps1 still defines as spx-scanner — the container the ZIP replaced on 3 August. EventBridge invokes spx-scanner-zip. Any setting changed since the switch reported success and never reached a running cycle.

Test-LambdaExists made it quieter still: it took no parameter and always tested $FuncName, so build-zip.ps1’s existing Test-LambdaExists $ZipFuncName guard was silently checking the wrong function. Both are fixed — the helper takes a name, and sync-env.ps1 defaults to the ZIP.

Still open: the rest of deploy/ resolves $FuncName to the retired container — invoke.ps1, alarms.ps1 and schedule.ps1 among them. The live alarms and schedules point at the ZIP only because they were repointed by hand, so the scripts and the account now disagree.

Phase 7

The write path, and what a post-close deploy hides — 5 August

Yesterday shipped four correct changes and one of them could not be saved. The defect was in production for fifteen hours and twenty consecutive cycles before anything noticed, because every one of those cycles ran outside market hours.

5 Aug Every in-market cycle with a candidate died on the writeoutage

save_prediction does SpreadSuggestion(prediction_id=…, **sp). The strategy’s output dict is that table’s schema, whether or not anyone intended it to be. r58 began emitting premium_edge from both spread dicts and added risk and prompt to item_scores — but never the matching column on spread_suggestions. Every write carrying a candidate raised TypeError: 'premium_edge' is an invalid keyword argument.

  • 06:10 — clean; pre-open, so prediction deferred and the write never reached.
  • 06:55 — three attempts, all failed. First in-market cycle since the deploy.
  • 07:40 — failed, then a retry succeeded: it found no candidate, so the loop that breaks had nothing to iterate. The failure was conditional on the scan producing something, which is why it is not visible as a flat outage.
  • 08:25 — three attempts, all failed.

Seven failed invocations, each having already paid for collection, per-item scoring and a full Opus-5 synthesis before reaching the write. The spx-scanner-errors alarm fired at 08:31 PT.

Root causethe ORM model and the strategy output drifted apart in 7d9e065, and nothing in the test suite compared them. Not a deployment fault: the same code fails identically on a laptop, in market hours, with a candidate.
5 Aug Why twenty green cycles preceded it — the lesson worth more than the fix

The deploy landed 17:38 PT, after the close. Outside RTH run_once defers prediction and returns before the write, so the entire overnight run exercised collection, scoring, synthesis and the regime read on code that could not save a prediction. Twenty consecutive clean cycles, none of which touched the broken line.

A post-close deploy buys no confidence in anything downstream of the market-hours branch. Those green cycles were read as evidence yesterday — the combined roadmap said so in as many words — and they were not evidence of anything except that the deferral path works. The scanner has two materially different execution paths and only one of them runs at night.

Applies toevery future deploy that touches prediction, scanning, marking or paper trading. Either ship in market hours, or invoke once by hand against the live function before the next open and read the result.
5 Aug Three columns, and the test that makes this class of bug fail on the laptopfixed

premium_edge added to SpreadSuggestion, plus call_short_strike and call_long_strike — the same bug one keyword further on. 11 Aug Zero downside breaches in 43 windows — and the calibrator is built not to believe it

The first measurement of the tail estimates against realised moves, and it is lopsided in a way nobody predicted:

  • Downside: 0 breaches in 43 windows. Mean excursion 0.05 EM, maximum 0.28 — the index never came close. Stated downside_risk ran 25–42% throughout.
  • Upside: also 0 past 1.0 EM, but mean 0.43 and max 0.98. Five of 43 windows cleared 0.9 EM. The live tail is the one the model consistently rated lower — stated upside_risk averaged 0.22 against downside’s 0.35.

Read naively the downside line says the estimate is roughly ten times too high and put spreads should be sold freely. That reading is how premium sellers die. 43 windows sampled 45 minutes apart across 14 observed days is not 43 observations; at a 4-day window it deflates to about three, all of them inside one calm, rising, contango regime. With zero breaches in three effective windows the upper bound on the true rate is still 0.79. The absence of a rare event over a quiet fortnight is very nearly no evidence at all — and a quiet fortnight is exactly the setup for the tail it would teach you to sell.

So the rule is not “match the point estimate”. A stated value stands unless it falls outside the interval the data supports; when it does, a correction may go to the point estimate when tightening but only to the near edge of the interval when loosening. Understating a tail costs the account; overstating one costs a trade, and the calibrator is allowed to be wrong only in the second direction.

result0.35 sits inside [0.00, 0.79], so the factor is 1.000 and nothing moves. That is the correct answer, not a failure to learn. As windows accumulate the interval narrows on its own, and if the downside really is this quiet the correction arrives — slowly, bounded per refit, and without a calm fortnight ever being able to stampede it.

When is this finished?

added 9 Aug 2026

Fifteen days of this log read as an unbroken sequence of defects, each fix revealing the next binding constraint. That pattern has an end, but only once you separate the two things the scanner produces — because they are not close to the same maturity and only one of them is on the critical path.

 Track A — the hazard signalTrack B — the tradeable spread
What it is per-side tail estimates and event timing, consumed by Sopana as a veto only a ranked, priced credit spread the signal arm can open
Depends on collectors, scorer, synthesis, econ calendar, uptime all of Track A, plus delta band, buffer, RoR floor, POP, liquidity, edge score
Failure mode fewer Sopana trades. A veto can only subtract. a book of negative-EV trades
Blocked by Gate 8 — 10 clean trading days. 2 / 10, running from 6 Aug. a measurement that says these trades should not be taken
Ready ~19 Aug, then shadow-logged from 21 Aug not date-bound

Track A can be called stable on three conditions, none of which needs a single signal trade.

  • Gate 8 closes. Ten consecutive clean trading days. Two are banked; ~19 August if nothing aborts. This is the only date-bound item in the whole project.
  • The tail gate is observed firing. It is 0-for-61 on blocking either side. A gate that has never said no is unvalidated in the direction that matters, and the veto is built entirely out of it. Until it refuses something, “correctly permissive” and “wired to always pass” are indistinguishable — the identical confusion that hid confidence_gate for a fortnight and event_risk for the life of the function.
  • Event timing is exported as a horizon, not a verdict. Open, cheap, and on the critical path for 21 August.

Track B has no completion date, and 9 August is the first day that looked like an answer rather than a delay. The honest reading of the premium measurement is that the trades on offer in this regime are not worth taking — 23 of 25 negative-EV once POP stops being borrowed from the option’s own price. If that survives contact with a richer regime, the signal arm is not broken and never was; it is a strategy correctly declining a bad tape, and it will open positions when IV/RV crosses 1 and stays there.

The standing instruction is therefore unchanged and now has arithmetic behind it rather than caution: do not lower min_edge_score, and do not raise premium_weight, to manufacture a treatment group. The one thing that would genuinely change the picture is a measured POP, and that is accumulating from r72 onward.

What would mean it is not stable, and is worth naming in advance. Track A breaks if a cycle is lost (the clock resets), if the tail estimates go constant, or if the econ calendar goes stale without saying so — the failure mode with no alarm, and the one that has already happened twice under different names. None of those are Track B problems, and none of them are fixed by anything on the watch list.
